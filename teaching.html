Teaching & Mentoring

Sharing the universe.

Beyond research, I am devoted to being a learner-centered instructor — and to sharing what I develop freely with the astronomy teaching community. Below are the active-learning activities I use in my courses, free to download and adapt.

Philosophy on learning

In all of my classes I focus on creating an active learning environment, making use of techniques including peer instruction, flipped classrooms, and in-class activities. I share the materials I've developed freely, in the hope they're useful to other instructors and students.

Astronomy 301 — Introduction to Astronomy

A general-education introduction to astronomy. Downloadable materials:

  • Course syllabus: in-person section · web-based section
  • Astrophysical Scales activity — a group activity using scaled models to grasp relative distances across the solar system, galaxy, and universe.
  • Phases of Venus activity — students explore heliocentric vs. geocentric models using phase observations.
  • Dark Matter activity — a "jigsaw expert" exercise with three images showing the evidence for dark matter.
  • Photometric Redshifts activity — distance estimation using real galaxy images and filter curves.

I also recommend the Lecture-Tutorials for Introductory Astronomy and the resources of the Center for Astronomy Education.

Astronomy 358 — Galaxies and the Universe

An upper-division course on galaxies and cosmology. Downloadable materials:

  • Course syllabus (Spring 2017)
  • Distance to Andromeda activity — students replicate Edwin Hubble's measurement using Cepheid variables. [data]
  • Stellar Populations activity — combine individual stellar spectra to understand integrated galaxy light. [spectra]
  • Photometric Redshifts activity (advanced) — students sort real Hubble-filtered galaxies by redshift. [data package]
  • Lyman-alpha Emission activity — students predict the emergent line profile after photons scatter through neutral gas.

Astronomy 376R — Research Methods

A hands-on course introducing undergraduates to the tools of astronomical research — UNIX and the command line, literature search with NASA ADS, image analysis with DS9, and scientific Python. Course syllabus.

Selected in-class activities, free to use and adapt:

  • UNIX & the command line — intro activity · reference sheet
  • Literature search with NASA ADS — activity
  • Image analysis with DS9 — part 1 · part 2 (HST imaging data available on request)
  • Scientific Python (Jupyter notebooks) — intro to Python · reading data tables · plotting · sample data

Mentoring & undergraduate research

I mentor students at every level. Undergraduates join our Galaxy Evolution Vertically Integrated Project (GEVIP), where they work on a sustained research project over roughly five semesters. Graduate students and postdocs in the research group lead their own projects at the frontier of early-universe astronomy.
